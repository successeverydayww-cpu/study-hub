How to construct a regular hexagon

Construct a regular hexagon of a given side, and know the other regular shapes (pentagon, octagon) the same exam can ask for.

📝 Step-by-step method

  1. Method 1 (circle method): draw a circle whose radius equals the side of the hexagon.
  2. Keep the compass at that same radius. Place the point on the circle and mark an arc across it. Move to that arc point and repeat. You get 6 points around the circle.
  3. Join the six points in order with a ruler. That is your regular hexagon.
  4. Method 2 (T-square and 30°/60° set square): draw a circle of the same radius, then use the 60° set square from the horizontal centre line to draw the six sides.
  5. Across corners = 2 × side (the circle diameter). Across flats = 0.866 × across corners.
  6. Pentagon: divide the circle into 5 using the angle 72° (360 ÷ 5). Octagon: 45° steps. General rule: 360° ÷ number of sides.
  7. Always check: all six sides equal, all internal angles 120°.
Exam tip: Hexagon side = circle radius. This one fact builds the hexagonal symbol, the bolt head and the nut.

📝 Step-by-step method

  1. Let D = major diameter of the bolt (e.g. 50 mm).
  2. Across corners of head and nut = about 2D (your paper uses 1.44Ø, so follow your paper).
  3. Head thickness = 0.7D (your paper: 0.5Ø). Nut thickness = 0.8D (your paper: 0.6Ø). Always use the figures in the question first.
  4. Draw the top view first: a circle of the across-corners size, then the hexagon inside it, with the corner pointing along the axis.
  5. Project down to the front view: the head shows three faces (a middle face and two foreshortened side faces).
  6. Draw the 30° chamfer: a circle of diameter = across flats, then arcs at the corners of the head and nut.
  7. Add the thread length on the shank, with thread lines (minor diameter) and the end chamfer at 45°.
  8. Label with a leader the hexagonal symbol.
Exam tip: Use the proportions given in your own question paper. In ENG 112 they are 0.92, 3.6, 2.2, 1.44, 0.5 and 0.6 times the major diameter.

📝 Step-by-step method

  1. Your Figure 1E-1 shows every term the examiner wants. Copy its labels exactly: external thread (on the bolt), internal thread (in the nut), axis, crest, root, pitch, depth of thread, thread angle, and the three diameters: minor, pitch and major.
  2. Draw the centre line (axis) first, then the bolt shank either side of it.
  3. Mark the major diameter (outer crest to crest) and minor diameter (root to root) as two parallel pairs of lines.
  4. The pitch is the distance from one crest to the next crest along the axis. Mark equal pitch divisions.
  5. Depth of thread = (major diameter − minor diameter) ÷ 2.
  6. The pitch diameter sits halfway down the flank, between crest and root diameters.
  7. Draw the V-shape with an angle of thread of 60° (ISO metric).
  8. Label external thread on the bolt and internal thread inside the nut. Label crest and root on the profile.
  9. Add title block, neat lettering, and dimension lines.
Exam tip: Figure 1E-1 is your answer key for Q1a. Reproduce it with every label and leader line, one mark per term.

📝 Step-by-step method

  1. Work every dimension out first, with Ø = 50 mm.
  2. Minor diameter = 0.92 × 50 = 46 mm.
  3. Axis length = 3.6 × 50 = 180 mm.
  4. Thread length = 2.2 × 50 = 110 mm.
  5. Nut/bolt-head across-corners diameter = 1.44 × 50 = 72 mm.
  6. Bolt head width (thickness) = 0.5 × 50 = 25 mm.
  7. Nut width (thickness) = 0.6 × 50 = 30 mm.
  8. Draw the front view of bolt and nut, then the top view as a hexagon inside a circle of 72 mm.
  9. Add chamfer arcs (30°) on the hex corners, the hexagonal symbol, thread lines, and the title block.
Exam tip: Do the calculations in a small table at the top of your sheet. The marks start with correct numbers: 46, 180, 110, 72, 25, 30.

Truncated pentagonal prism

A pentagonal prism, base edges 25 mm, length 50 mm, rests on its base with an edge of the base at 45° to the V.P. It is cut by a section plane V.T inclined at 30° to the H.P through a point 25 mm from the base along its axis. Develop the lateral surface of the truncated prism.

📝 Step-by-step method

  1. Draw the top view: a regular pentagon of 25 mm side, with one edge at 45° to the XY line. Number the corners.
  2. Project up for the front view: a rectangle 50 mm tall, using the pentagon corners as vertical lines.
  3. Mark the point on the axis 25 mm up from the base.
  4. Draw the cutting plane through that point at 30° to the XY line (it is a V.T, so it shows as a line in the front view).
  5. Mark where the plane cuts each of the five vertical edges (points a, b, c, d, e).
  6. For the development: draw a straight line = perimeter = 5 × 25 = 125 mm, divide into five 25 mm parts.
  7. Erect perpendiculars at each division. Their heights = the true cut heights taken from the front view.
  8. Join the tops with straight lines, and complete the base. This is the developed lateral surface.
Exam tip: The development line is 125 mm long. Height of each edge comes straight from the front view. Label corners A B C D E on both views.

📝 Step-by-step method

  1. Study Fig I: a stepped base (150 × 25), a raised block (110 × 60) with a rectangular pocket 30 deep.
  2. Choose the front view direction so the pocket and steps show clearly.
  3. Sectional front view: cut along the centre line through the pocket. Draw the cut face with 45° hatching lines, evenly spaced.
  4. Half sectional side view: half of the view shows the outside, the other half shows the cut. Divide with a centre line, not a thick line.
  5. Top view: draw the outline from above, show the pocket as a hidden/visible rectangle, and add the small holes in dotted lines if hidden.
  6. Line up views with projection lines. Keep the first-angle or third-angle layout consistent.
  7. Dimension neatly and add the title block.
Exam tip: Hatching is never drawn through ribs or solid thin parts. Cutting plane line goes on the other view with arrows.

📝 Step-by-step method

  1. Study Fig II: a base block (100 long), a raised step, an inclined bracket at about 45° with a circular hole (Ø30), and a small block.
  2. Front view in direction A: the profile that shows the slope of the bracket, the heights (25, 45, 60) and the circle.
  3. Side view: project across from the front view, showing depth (30, 40, 50).

  7. Draw the circle in isometric as an ellipse, hidden lines dotted in orthographic views only.

Development of right cylinders

Study Examples 5, 6 and 7: develop the surface of a right cylinder cut by an inclined plane, using 12 equal divisions of the right cross-section and the stretch-out line.

📝 Step-by-step method

  1. Draw the elevation of the cylinder and the cutting plane.
  2. Draw the right cross-section (a circle) and divide it into 12 equal divisions (every 30°). Number them 0 to 12.
  3. Project the 12 divisions up to the elevation as surface lines (TL). Each surface line meets the cutting plane at a point.
  4. Draw the stretch-out line at 90° to the axis. Its length = circumference = π × D. Mark 12 equal parts on it.
  5. Erect perpendiculars at each division. Take each length from the elevation (true length, TL).
  6. Join the tops with a smooth curve. The result is the sinusoidal developed surface.
  7. In Example 7 (oblique cylinder), the stretch-out line is drawn at 90° to the axis (the right cross-section), not to the base.
Exam tip: Stretch-out length = π × D. The curve is smooth, never straight-lined. Number divisions on both the circle and the stretch-out line.
