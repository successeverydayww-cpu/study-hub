Q1a · 10 marks

Bolt and nut terms

Using related instruments, draw a bolt and nut showing: external thread, internal thread, axis, major diameter, minor diameter, pitch diameter, pitch, crest, root, depth of thread and angle of thread.

🎥 Watch and learn

▶ Screw Thread Nomenclature | How to Draw
Open on YouTube

📝 Step-by-step method

  1. Draw the centre line (axis) first, then the bolt shank either side of it.
  2. Mark the major diameter (outer crest to crest) and minor diameter (root to root) as two parallel pairs of lines.
  3. The pitch is the distance from one crest to the next crest along the axis. Mark equal pitch divisions.
  4. Depth of thread = (major diameter − minor diameter) ÷ 2.
  5. The pitch diameter sits halfway down the flank, between crest and root diameters.
  6. Draw the V-shape with an angle of thread of 60° (ISO metric).
  7. Label external thread on the bolt and internal thread inside the nut. Label crest and root on the profile.
  8. Add title block, neat lettering, and dimension lines.
Exam tip: Terms examiners love: crest, root, pitch, depth, 60° angle. Label every term with a leader line, one mark each.
Q1b · 20 marks

Hex-head bolt and nut (50 mm)

Construct hex-head bolt and nut: major Ø = 50 mm, minor diameter = 0.92Ø, axis = 3.6Ø, thread length = 2.2Ø, nut diameter = 1.44Ø, bolt head width = 0.5Ø, nut width = 0.6Ø. Include the hexagonal symbol.

▶ Screw thread terms and drawing a bolt

📝 Step-by-step method

  1. Work every dimension out first, with Ø = 50 mm.
  2. Minor diameter = 0.92 × 50 = 46 mm.
  3. Axis length = 3.6 × 50 = 180 mm.
  4. Thread length = 2.2 × 50 = 110 mm.
  5. Nut/bolt-head across-corners diameter = 1.44 × 50 = 72 mm.
  6. Bolt head width (thickness) = 0.5 × 50 = 25 mm.
  7. Nut width (thickness) = 0.6 × 50 = 30 mm.
  8. Draw the front view of bolt and nut, then the top view as a hexagon inside a circle of 72 mm.
  9. Add chamfer arcs (30°) on the hex corners, the hexagonal symbol, thread lines, and the title block.
Exam tip: Do the calculations in a small table at the top of your sheet. The marks start with correct numbers: 46, 180, 110, 72, 25, 30.
Q2 · 15 marks

Truncated pentagonal prism

A pentagonal prism, base edges 25 mm, length 50 mm, rests on its base with an edge of the base at 45° to the V.P. It is cut by a section plane V.T inclined at 30° to the H.P through a point 25 mm from the base along its axis. Develop the lateral surface of the truncated prism.

📝 Step-by-step method

  1. Draw the top view: a regular pentagon of 25 mm side, with one edge at 45° to the XY line. Number the corners.
  2. Project up for the front view: a rectangle 50 mm tall, using the pentagon corners as vertical lines.
  3. Mark the point on the axis 25 mm up from the base.
  4. Draw the cutting plane through that point at 30° to the XY line (it is a V.T, so it shows as a line in the front view).
  5. Mark where the plane cuts each of the five vertical edges (points a, b, c, d, e).
  6. For the development: draw a straight line = perimeter = 5 × 25 = 125 mm, divide into five 25 mm parts.
  7. Erect perpendiculars at each division. Their heights = the true cut heights taken from the front view.
  8. Join the tops with straight lines, and complete the base. This is the developed lateral surface.
Exam tip: The development line is 125 mm long. Height of each edge comes straight from the front view. Label corners A B C D E on both views.

📝 Step-by-step method

  1. Study Fig I: a stepped base (150 × 25), a raised block (110 × 60) with a rectangular pocket 30 deep.
  2. Choose the front view direction so the pocket and steps show clearly.
  3. Sectional front view: cut along the centre line through the pocket. Draw the cut face with 45° hatching lines, evenly spaced.
  4. Half sectional side view: half of the view shows the outside, the other half shows the cut. Divide with a centre line, not a thick line.
  5. Top view: draw the outline from above, show the pocket as a hidden/visible rectangle, and add the small holes in dotted lines if hidden.
  6. Line up views with projection lines. Keep the first-angle or third-angle layout consistent.
  7. Dimension neatly and add the title block.
Exam tip: Hatching is never drawn through ribs or solid thin parts. Cutting plane line goes on the other view with arrows.

📝 Step-by-step method

  1. Study Fig II: a base block (100 long), a raised step, an inclined bracket at about 45° with a circular hole (Ø30), and a small block.
  2. Front view in direction A: the profile that shows the slope of the bracket, the heights (25, 45, 60) and the circle.
  3. Side view: project across from the front view, showing depth (30, 40, 50).
  4. Top view: project down from the front view, with widths matching the side view.
  5. Use 45° miter line to transfer depth between top and side view.
  6. Isometric drawing: draw axes at 30°. Measure real lengths along the three axes. Draw the base block first, then the step, then the inclined bracket and the block.
  7. Draw the circle in isometric as an ellipse, hidden lines dotted in orthographic views only.
Exam tip: Do orthographic views first, because they give you all the dimensions for the isometric drawing.
